# EgoWaste
Each year, over 100 million tons of potentially recoverable material in municipal solid waste (MSW) are lost to landfills and incineration plants in the U.S., highlighting the need for automated material characterization and sorting. However, existing waste datasets are often collected in controlled settings and fail to capture the visual and operational complexity of real-world MSW systems, where objects are cluttered, deformed, contaminated, deteriorated, and frequently occluded. We introduce EgoWaste, a hierarchical egocentric waste dataset collected from MSW disposal sites. Unlike static waste datasets, EgoWaste captures ego-centric multimodal data about experts in sorting MSW, including hand-object interactions, temporal manipulation cues, and speech. EgoWaste contains 25.66 hours of first-person audio-video, with 35,754 annotated frames and 39,083 active-object instances labeled with segmentation masks, bounding boxes, and hierarchical labels spanning 15 material categories and 56 fine-grained waste classes. To scale annotation, we develop a multimodal pipeline that aligns transcribed expert speech with hand detection, tracking, and hand-object interaction, allowing spoken labels to be associated with the item being sorted with limited manual effort. We validate this pipeline through manual audit and define baselines for hierarchical classification, object detection, and instance segmentation. EgoWaste provides a benchmark for material-aware waste understanding and supports the development of AI systems for waste-stream analysis and material recovery in real-world.
